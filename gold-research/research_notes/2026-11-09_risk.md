### 1. Volatility-based position sizing

**1a. Turtle "N" rule (Dennis/Eckhardt, 1983; published by Curtis Faith, *Original Turtle Trading Rules*, 2003)**
- Rule: N = 20-day exponential average of True Range (Wilder TR); Dollar Volatility = N × dollars-per-point; **Unit = 1% of account ÷ Dollar Volatility**; initial stop = **2N** from entry (so one unit stopped out = ~2% of account); add units every ½N; System 1 exit = 10-day low/high, System 2 exit = 20-day low/high; limits 4 units single market, 6 closely correlated, 10 loosely correlated, 12 one direction.
- Drawdown rule: for each **10% drawdown**, trade as if the account were **20% smaller** (so a 2% unit becomes 1.6%, then 1.28%).
- Example for gold: $100k account, N = $60/oz, 1 standard lot = 100 oz → Dollar Volatility = $6,000 → Unit = $1,000/$6,000 = **0.17 lot**; 2N stop = $120 → risk ≈ $2,000 (2%).
- Sources: Turtle rules themselves not fetchable; drawdown-rule wording confirmed in SNIPPET from https://www.turtletrader.com/recovery/ and https://www.thechartist.com.au/being-proactive-with-position-sizing/ ("decrease the size of their trading account by 20% each time the strategy drawdown reached −10%"); ATR definition READ IN FULL at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Average_true_range (TR = max[H−L, |H−C_prev|, |L−C_prev|]; ATR_t = (ATR_{t−1}(n−1)+TR_t)/n, Wilder default n = 14). Wikipedia Richard Dennis page (READ IN FULL, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dennis) confirms Turtles were taught to "cut position size during losing periods and to pyramid aggressively" and that "only 24% of total capital would be exposed at any one time".
- Evidence status: rules = published primary document (Faith 2003), confirmed here only via secondary snippets. Note the Wikipedia Turtle page is 404.

**1b. Van Tharp — R-multiples and the "percent risk" model (*Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom*, 1998/2007)**
- Definitions: **R = initial risk per unit = |entry − initial stop|**. Trade result in R-multiples = P&L ÷ R (stopped at initial stop = −1R). **Expectancy = mean R-multiple** = Σ(R_i)/n.
- Percent-risk model: **Position size = (Equity × risk%) ÷ R**. Tharp describes this as an anti-martingale model where "the first step … is to decide how much you are going to risk on each trade in terms of a percentage of your trading capital" (SNIPPET of a book summary, https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/207446). Tharp himself gives ≤1% as the conservative setting in the book; the specific "1%" sentence was not retrievable → **widely attributed but unverified** here. He calls position sizing the most important chapter (Ch. 14 in later editions) (SNIPPET, https://www.asktraders.com/learn-to-trade/risk-management/position-sizing-secrets/).
- Secondary definitions of R confirmed at https://members.bearbulltraders.com/trading-to-the-power-of-r/ (SNIPPET) and https://crosstrade.io/learn/performance-metrics/r-multiple (SNIPPET).

**1c. Ralph Vince — optimal f (*Portfolio Management Formulas*, 1990; *The Mathematics of Money Management*, 1992)**
- Formula: for trade results t_i and largest loss L (positive number), **HPR_i(f) = 1 + f·(t_i / L)**; **TWR(f) = Π HPR_i(f)**; **optimal f = argmax TWR(f), f ∈ [0,1)**; contracts = account ÷ (L / f).
- Why practitioners use a fraction: (i) f is anchored to the historical largest loss, which is only known after the fact—one new outlier shifts every position size; (ii) drawdown at full f "routinely exceed[s] levels most traders … would abandon, frequently more than half the account" (SNIPPET, https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/optimal-f/); a forum article cites 50–70% expected max drawdown at full f (SNIPPET, https://nexusfi.com/a/risk-management/optimal-f-position-sizing, unverified); (iii) Vince discussed **"half f" (0.5 × optimal f)** as a compromise that in his example "reduced variability and expected drawdown by approximately half" (SNIPPET, https://jp.tradingview.com/script/b4HV7iJR-Optimal-F-by-Ralph-Vince-Position-Sizing-and-Risk-Analysis/). Academic restatement of the TWR definition with the maximal-loss normaliser: SNIPPET of https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1612.02985 and https://www.instmath.rwth-aachen.de/Preprints/maierpaape20131008.pdf (Maier-Paape, who also notes Vince later added a drawdown-probability constraint).
- Evidence: formula = primary (Vince); drawdown numbers = secondary, unverified.

**1d. Kelly criterion and half-Kelly (Kelly 1956; Thorp)**
- Binary form: **f* = p − q/b** (p win prob, q = 1−p, b = win/loss payoff ratio). Wikipedia's general form: f = p/l − q/g (l = fraction lost, g = fraction gained). Example: p = 0.45, b = 2 → f* = 0.45 − 0.55/2 = **17.5%** of equity—far above anything a CFD trader should use, which is the point.
- Continuous (Gaussian) form: **f = (μ − r)/σ²** (μ = expected excess return, σ² = variance).
- Fractional Kelly: "Even Kelly supporters usually argue for fractional Kelly" (READ IN FULL, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_criterion, citing Thorp, *Wilmott*, May 2008, "The Kelly Criterion: Part I"). **Half-Kelly growth ≈ 0.75 × full-Kelly growth; betting 2× Kelly drives growth to zero**: MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba table lists "relative growth rate of 0.75 at half Kelly" and "betting exactly twice the Kelly wager drives growth to the risk-free rate" (SNIPPET of https://escholarship.org/content/qt5mr5k8qj/qt5mr5k8qj.pdf — MacLean/Thorp/Ziemba, "Good and bad properties of the Kelly criterion"; also https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/Good_Bad_Kelly.pdf).
- Evidence: formulas = primary (Kelly/Thorp via Wikipedia); 0.75 figure = academic paper, SNIPPET only.

- **The5ers High Stakes**: **5% daily loss**, **10% max drawdown** (static from initial balance); breach = account closed (SNIPPET https://proptradingvibes.com/blog/the5ers-drawdown-rules ; https://tradingfunder.com/firms/the5ers/). - **FundedNext Stellar 2-Step**: **5% daily / 10% overall**, static, reset 00:00 UTC; Stellar 1-Step 3% daily / 6% max; FundedNext's own blog (fundednext.com/blog/stellar-1-step-vs-2-step-challenge, SNIPPET) confirms "5% daily / 10% overall". FundedNext also imposes a **3% maximum open risk** across all running CFD positions, with no-stop positions counted at full risk after 3 minutes (SNIPPET https://ptv-dev.pages.dev/blog/fundednext-risk-management — unverified against official terms).
- Generic daily-limit mechanics (daily vs max loss): https://traderssecondbrain.com/prop-firm-rules/daily-loss-vs-max-loss (SNIPPET).

**"Three strikes" daily stop**: Rule = stop trading for the session after **3 consecutive losing trades** (variant: any 3 losses). Origin untraceable; earliest attributed practitioner statement is a CBOT pit trader (handle @jstnbrg) at https://nexusfi.com/a/risk-management/consecutive-loss-protocols (SNIPPET). Also https://tradefundrr.com/blog/the-three-strike-rule-for-consecutive-losses (SNIPPET). **Status: widely used folk rule, no primary source, no test.**

**Equity-curve trading**: Rule = compute equity curve of closed trades; trade full size only when equity > SMA(equity, 30–50 trades), else stop or trade minimum (SNIPPET https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/equity-curve-based-throttling.md). **No peer-reviewed or out-of-sample test found**; practitioner threads report stopping entirely beat half-sizing in one case and warn "it is very easy to curve fit an average to anything – including a historical equity curve" (SNIPPET https://www.prorealcode.com/topic/what-do-you-look-for-in-a-backtest-result ; https://www.wealth-lab.com/Discussion/Using-the-Trading-the-Equity-Curve-PosSizer-for-discretionary-trading-10187).

**Size reduction after X% drawdown**: only documented rule with a primary source is the Turtle "−10% DD → trade as if account 20% smaller" (§1a) and PTJ's qualitative "keep reducing position size" (§2). A "halve size after X%" rule has **no traceable primary source**.

### 6. Expectancy and sample size (math)

**Trades needed to distinguish mean expectancy μ (in R) from zero:**
n = (z · σ / μ)² — from the standard margin-of-error formula n = 4Z²σ²/W² with E = W/2 (READ IN FULL https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_size_determination; z = 1.96 for 95% two-sided quoted there).

Worked example, σ = 1.2 R, μ = 0.1 R:
- 90% one-sided (z = 1.2816): n = (1.2816 × 12)² ≈ **237 trades**
- 90% two-sided / 95% one-sided (z = 1.645): n = (1.645 × 12)² ≈ **390 trades**
- 95% two-sided (z = 1.96): n ≈ **553 trades**
Interpretation: a 0.1 R edge with typical trade dispersion needs several hundred trades before the mean is statistically distinguishable from zero; sizing rules must assume the edge may be zero during that period.

**Probability of at least k consecutive losses in n trades** (loss prob q = 1 − win rate, p = win rate), exact recursion:
P(n) = 0 for n < k; P(k) = q^k; **P(n) = P(n−1) + [1 − P(n−k−1)] · p · q^k** for n > k.
Computed values (exact): win 40%, k = 8, n = 200 → **0.75**; win 40%, k = 10, n = 200 → 0.38; win 50%, k = 7, n = 100 → 0.32; win 50%, k = 10, n = 250 → 0.11; win 35%, k = 10, n = 200 → 0.62. The naive 1 − (1 − q^k)^(n−k+1) materially overstates these (e.g. 0.96 vs 0.75). Implication: at 1% risk and 40% win rate, an 8-loss streak (−8R) is the expected case in a 200-trade year, so the daily/weekly stop and the max-loss buffer must accommodate ≥8–10R.

### 7. Correlation / concentration

- Single-instrument intraday trading has zero cross-sectional diversification: every open position is exposed to the same price process, so k simultaneous XAUUSD positions = one position k× larger (Kovner's "eight highly correlated positions … one position that is eight times as large", §3). Turtle limits formalise this: max 4 units per market, 12 in one direction (§1a).
- Prop-rule concentration caps found: FundedNext **3% total open risk** (§5); Hantec broker **3% maximum open risk** on floating losses (SNIPPET https://help.htrader.hmarkets.com/support/solutions/articles/158000444286-what-is-maximum-open-risk-). No open-risk cap found for FTMO or The5ers; their binding constraint is the 5% daily loss.
