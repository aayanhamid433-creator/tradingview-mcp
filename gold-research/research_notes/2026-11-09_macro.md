## 1. Paul Tudor Jones (Tudor Investment Corp.)

**Market / horizon:** Macro futures (gold futures, rates, FX, equity indices); multi-week to 12–24 month thematic positions. Not an intraday gold trader.

**Rules as documented (all secondhand):**
- **200-day MA filter.** Attributed to the Tony Robbins interview in *Money: Master the Game* (2014): "My metric for everything I look at is the 200-day moving average of closing prices." On 1987: it "had gone under the 200-day moving target. At the very top of the crash, I was flat." Gap: the source does not specify the instrument the MA applies to beyond "everything," nor whether exits use a close-through or an intraday touch, nor any re-entry rule. The popular phrase "nothing good happens below the 200-day" appeared only on a quote-aggregator page with no citation; treat as **unverified paraphrase**.
- **5:1 reward/risk.** Widely attributed line: he looks for roughly $5 of potential gain per $1 risked, so "I can be wrong 80% of the time" and still not lose. No source found pinning it to a specific page of the Robbins book or *Market Wizards*; wording varies across sites. Gap: how the $5 target and $1 stop are measured (ATR, chart level, time) is nowhere specified.
- **Defense / no averaging losers.** "Losers average losers" is the sheet of paper on his office wall in the 1987 PBS documentary *Trader*; the 2009 speech rendering is "Don't ever average losers." The "play defense" line is tied to the 200-day rule: if price is below it, "you get out. You play defense." Gap: no position-sizing or stop-distance rule is given.

**Gold calls:**
- **June 12, 2019, Bloomberg TV (Vonnie Quinn):** gold is his "favorite trade for the next 12–24 months"; "if it goes through $1,400, it goes to $1,700 rather quickly"; framed as "first rate cut 101" (long rates, long gold, later short dollar) plus trade-war recession risk. Gold was ~$1,340 at the time and traded above $1,700 by spring 2020 — directionally correct (my price check, not sourced).
- **May 2020 "The Great Monetary Inflation" letter (with Lorenzo Giorgianni):** "In a low-carry world, gold remains a very attractive hedge against the Great Monetary Inflation"; the fund may hold "a low single-digit percentage" in bitcoin futures; "the best profit-maximizing strategy is to own the fastest horse ... my bet is it will be Bitcoin."

**Performance evidence:** Tudor BVI Global is a real, long-running regulated fund (Form ADV cited on Wikipedia; 1987 +125.9%, 1990 +87.4%, ~5%/yr 2010–12 per NYT). But **no public attribution of fund P&L to gold exists**; the 2019 call's outcome is a directionally correct public call, not an audited gold return. Label: fund record *independently substantiated in aggregate*; **gold-specific: no usable performance record found.** Note the 2020 flagship was reported down ~2% through mid-August 2020 despite the gold rally.

**Transferability to intraday XAUUSD:** Only the 200-day (or scaled-down equivalent) as a long-only/short-only regime filter and the 5:1 asymmetry as a trade-selection screen. No entry trigger.

## 2. Stanley Druckenmiller (Duquesne Family Office)

**Market / horizon:** GLD ETF, Barrick (ABX/GOLD), GDX, Newmont, plus (undisclosed) gold futures; holding periods of quarters to ~2 years.

**13F evidence (regulated filings, as reported):**
- Q2 2015: 2.8–2.88M GLD shares (~$300–324M), reported as his largest position (~20% of 13F equities per a gold-industry commentator; treat that % with caution).
- Nov 2016 (CNBC, Nov 10): sold all gold on election night: "All the reasons I owned it for the last couple of years seem to be ending." Bought some back by May 2017.
- Q2 2020: 2.88M GLD (~$300M); Barrick ~$115M in top-10. Q3 2020: +581k Barrick. Q4 2020 (filed Feb 16, 2021): sold ~1.25M Barrick and ~1.8M GDX.
- Nov 9, 2020 CNBC: owns bitcoin but "far more gold"; gold is a devaluation/dollar bet; expects a steady 3–4 year dollar decline.
- Q4 2023/Q1 2024: new Barrick and Newmont positions reported; May 2023 Sohn: "lukewarm" on gold given hard-landing risk.

**Stated logic:** gold trades off the dollar and rates — 2021 Bowdoin interview (secondhand): if inflation rises "you may not want to own gold because interest rates will go up and gold trades off interest rates." No explicit real-rate threshold found. Gap: he never publishes a rule; the 2016 exit shows he trades gold off a *growth/real-rate regime read*, not a level.

**Sizing:** Lost Tree Club speech, Jan 18, 2015: Soros's lesson is that "when you have tremendous conviction on a trade, you have to go for the jugular"; "put all your eggs in one basket and watch the basket very carefully"; "bulls make money, bears make money, pigs get slaughtered — I'm here to tell you I was a pig." Gap: no numeric sizing rule.

**Performance evidence:** Duquesne ~30%/yr 1981–2010 with no down year — **self-reported / widely cited** (Wikipedia repeats it; no audited document found). The family office's 13Fs are regulated but show only long US equity/ETF positions, not P&L, so **gold-specific P&L: no usable record**; what is substantiated is the *timing* of his GLD/Barrick entries and exits (2015 buy, Nov 2016 sell near the post-election low, 2020 hold, Q4 2020 trim).

**Transferability:** regime filter only — be long-biased gold when dollar and real yields are falling, flat/short-biased when a growth/rate-hike regime starts; concentration-when-right as a sizing principle.

## 3. George Soros / Quantum Fund

**Market / horizon:** GLD, iShares Gold Trust, GLD call options, Barrick shares; quarters to ~1 year.

**Record:**
- **Davos, Jan 2010:** "When interest rates are low we have conditions for asset bubbles to develop ... The ultimate asset bubble is gold." Sept 2010 Reuters clarification: "I called gold the 'ultimate bubble' which means that it may be going higher, but it is certainly not safe."
- He **bought while calling it a bubble**: 5.24M GLD (~$650M) at June 30, 2010; 4.72M GLD at Dec 31, 2010; plus ~5M iShares Gold Trust (total gold ETFs ~$774M).
- **Q1 2011 13F:** GLD cut from 4.72M to ~49,400 shares ($6.9M); iShares position liquidated. Gold peaked ~$1,920 in Sept 2011, roughly 5–6 months later, so the exit was **early by ~25%** — a documented mistimed call (he did not catch the top; he avoided the 2013 crash).
- **Q1 2016 13F:** 19.4M Barrick (~$264M, new) and calls on 1.05M GLD (~$123M). **Q2 2016:** Barrick cut to 1.07M, GLD to 240k — profit-taking after the H1 2016 rally; Barrick had roughly doubled.

**Rules:** none published. Reflexivity thesis ("bubble can go higher"); position management is pure discretion.

**Performance evidence:** Quantum's aggregate record is well documented, but **gold-specific P&L is not disclosed**; 13Fs substantiate only direction and timing. Label: *timing independently substantiated via 13F; returns self-reported/unknown.*

**Transferability:** the one reusable idea is "low real rates = bubble conditions = stay long until the regime changes," i.e. a macro bias filter. Nothing intraday.

## 4. John Paulson (Paulson & Co. Gold Fund / PFR Gold Fund)

**Market / horizon:** Launched Jan 2010; gold miners, bullion-based derivatives, GLD (31.5M shares, ~$4.4B at peak 2011); multi-year hold. Most Paulson funds also offered gold-denominated share classes.

**Thesis (quoted):** "We view gold as a currency, not a commodity"; central-bank balance-sheet expansion "will eventually cause inflation." No exit rule; no stop.

**Returns (all press reports citing investor letters — self-reported, Bloomberg/Reuters relayed):**
- 2010: +35%.
- 2011: Bloomberg Dec 2011: −10.5%; Institutional Investor later: −35.96%. **Conflict unresolved.** Advantage Fund −32% (dollar class) / −13% (gold class).
- 2012: −14.2% (II).
- 2013: −27% April, −13% May, −23% June (−65% YTD), −16% Sept (−62% YTD), ~−63% mid-Dec; **full-year 2013 reported as −71%** (Bloomberg, Feb 2014). Fund had shrunk to ~$300–350M, mostly Paulson's own money.

**Performance evidence label:** *self-reported* (investor letters leaked to press; no audited gold-fund figures in public). It is the clearest documented **losing** gold record among these names: a long-only inflation thesis with no risk control through a 28% drop in gold in 2013.

**Transferability:** negative example — a fundamental thesis without a price-based invalidation is not usable intraday or otherwise.

## 5. Ray Dalio (Bridgewater / All Weather)

**This is allocation, not trading.** All Weather (1996) is risk-parity; the popular public version is ~30% stocks / 40% long bonds / 15% intermediate bonds / 7.5% gold / 7.5% commodities (Wikipedia's Bridgewater page renders a variant with 10% gold). Public recommendations: **Aug 9–10, 2017** LinkedIn/client note "Risks Are Rising": "5–10% of assets in gold" (North Korea, debt ceiling). **Jan 21, 2020, CNBC at Davos:** "Cash is trash ... get out of cash," plus "you have to have a certain amount of gold in your portfolio." **2025 (Greenwich Economic Forum / Kitco Oct 31, 2025):** "somewhere between 10% and 15%" / "5–15% depending on the rest of the portfolio." **Feb 2026 13F:** Dalio Family Office ~$503M US equities, >75% in gold ETFs (regulated filing, Bloomberg report via Wikipedia).

**Rules:** none for timing. **Evidence:** Bridgewater is audited but publishes no gold attribution; **gold-specific: no usable performance record.** **Transferability:** none for intraday; at most a reminder that his view is permanent structural long.

## 6. Peter Brandt (Factor LLC)

**Market / horizon:** Gold futures (GC), GLD, occasionally miners; classical Edwards & Magee patterns on weekly/daily charts; trades last days to several months.

**Rules (closest to reproducible of anyone here):**
- **Risk per trade:** his own Nov 2017 tweet thread: risk limited to **1% of capital max per trade**, size derived from the stop distance. A March 2017 member Q&A asks why he caps "about 100 bps." Reports of 0.25–0.75% (forex.in.rs) are third-party and unconfirmed; the "0.5%" figure in the brief was **not found in a primary source**.
- **Stops at pattern invalidation / "Last Day Rule":** from *Diary of a Professional Commodity Trader* (2011): initial stop is placed just beyond the extreme of the breakout day (the "Last Day Rule"); stop may be moved to a new Last Day Rule created by a continuation pattern. Gap: no fixed tick buffer specified.
- **Targets:** minimum measured move = pattern height from the breakout point (stated in the book and his gold PDFs: "Chart patterns are extremely reliable in Gold," Feb 2016 PDF title snippet).
- **"Popcorn trade":** a trade that reaches a significant profit and is then held until it round-trips back to breakeven or a loss; he explicitly tries to eliminate these (take partial profits / tighten stops once a target is approached).
- **Gold calls:** Aug 2024: inverse head-and-shoulders, targets **$2,800 then $3,200** (both subsequently hit). His site posts periodic "Chart history of Gold market" PDFs (Feb 2016, Oct 2023) grading past patterns. Also on record with failed calls (Dow 4,000 in 2011; Carnival bankruptcy call retracted in 2012, per forum critics).

**Performance evidence:** Factor LLC claims 41.56% average annual compounded return based on IRS tax reporting (period described as 1981–2010, "audited in 2011"). An EliteTrader thread (Apr–May 2018) alleges the word "audited" was replaced by "attestation" and that only ~18 years of tax returns underlie it (he stopped trading ~1995–2006). Neither the attestation document nor the retraction could be fetched. Label: **self-reported with partial third-party attestation; not independently verified**; no gold-only sub-record exists.

**Transferability:** highest of the group. The 1%-risk / stop-beyond-breakout-bar / pattern-height-target / anti-popcorn exit rules port directly to any timeframe, including intraday XAUUSD, as entry and risk templates.

## 7. Larry Williams

**Market / horizon:** Commodity futures including gold; swing trades of 2–5 days using COT + seasonals + Williams %R for timing.

**Rules:**
- **COT Index** (from *Trade Stocks and Commodities with the Insiders*, 2005, as reproduced in TradingView scripts citing the book): `COT Index = (Current commercial net − lowest net over lookback) / (highest net − lowest net) × 100`, lookback **3 years** (some implementations use 26 weeks or 6 months). Bullish when commercials' index is **above 75 (some say 74 or 80)**, bearish **below 25 (26/20)**. Threshold conflict not resolved because the book itself could not be fetched. Gap: Williams explicitly says COT sets *bias* and must be combined with a trend-change trigger; the trigger (often %R or a bar pattern) is not standardized in public sources.
- **Seasonals:** states August is historically a good month to buy gold (Mail & Guardian snippet); 2026 StockCharts forecast reportedly combined cycles with "commercials adding while open interest declines = bullish" and "buy setup, not yet long, awaiting trend change."
- **Williams %R** (10-period) as overbought/oversold timing.

**Performance evidence:** 1987 Robbins World Cup Championship: $10,000 → $1,137,600 (11,376%) in real-money, contest-verified account — **independently substantiated** for that one account (Wikipedia). But NFA proceedings (case 88BCC00035, settled June 8, 1990, $13,000 fine, no findings) quoted on EliteTrader state that in **Q1 1987 his managed customer accounts lost $6,122,281 while his personal accounts gained $902,599**. So the contest return is real but not representative of client results. One student PDF gives 1,091% — treat as an error. **Gold-specific P&L: none found.**

**Transferability:** COT Index is a weekly bias filter (works for XAUUSD via GC data); seasonals are a secondary weak filter; %R is one of the few intraday-portable timing tools here.

## 8. Jim Rogers and Peter Schiff (commentators)

- **Jim Rogers:** long-only physical gold/silver since the 2000s, "never sold," buys on sharp dips (named $1,300/$1,200 in 2013); Quantum 1973–80 record (+4,200%) is pre-gold era. **No trading rules, no post-1980 performance record.** Called gold down to $900–1,200 in 2013 (partially right). Not usable except as sentiment.
- **Peter Schiff:** Euro Pacific Capital/SchiffGold; 2009 "$5,000 gold" call (eventually exceeded in 2026, 16+ years later); permabull through the 2011–2015 bear; clients reported down 40–70% in 2008 (WSJ/Shedlock); Euro Pacific Bank liquidated 2022, $300k fine. **No documented trading rules; no audited record; third-party scorecard 5/17 predictions correct.** Not usable.
